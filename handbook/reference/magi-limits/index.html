MAGI's limits

The sizes, counts and rates MAGI holds to, and everything it refuses, with what it says when it does. A limit here is a promise too: what is under it is taken, and what is over it is refused before anything is written, never cut short. What MAGI promises says what is kept; What the machine remembers is the chapter on using it.

A store

storesone per account in /etc/passwd, at /var/lib/magi/stores/UID, and the system's at /var/lib/magi/stores/system. A uid with no account is refused: uid N is no account in /etc/passwd, and only an account has a store.
a store's budget10% of the filesystem holding the stores, unless /etc/magi/budgets says otherwise: one UID-or-user-or-* AMOUNT a line, the amount in bytes with K, M, G or T, or a percentage. The file must be root's and writable by nobody else, or it is ignored.
at its budgeta batch that would pass it is refused, naming the budget and where it came from, and a batch that only closes a writer's attachment is still taken. Nothing is evicted to make room. A new stream's byte cap is held to what the budget has left.
writersone: magid. Stores are not shared across machines, and one store is one unit of atomicity: a batch spans streams of one store, never two stores.
the formatformat 1. A store from before the freeze is set aside, not read (What MAGI promises).

Streams

a nameNAMESPACE/NAME, lower-case letters, digits, -, _ and ., with any number of /-separated parts after the first. Ids are the store's to give, never a program's.
a declarationnames every column: kind, writer, signing (unsigned), trust, retention, a consumer, idempotency, durability, schema, and a view's plan. One that leaves out a consumer, a byte cap or a writer's program is refused.
declared againexactly as it is, it answers with the stream as declared and writes nothing; differing in anything, it is refused.
signingonly unsigned is taken; a key is refused until signing ships.
retentiona byte cap, required; a history stream may add a record cap of at least one. A state stream has no record cap: its byte cap bounds its live keys and their values, and a change past it is refused.
who writes itonly the program that declared it, as the kernel names it. A writer cannot send a state change of kind redact, which only a redaction writes.

Records and keys

a history recordat most 16 MiB. Larger data belongs in a file the record names.
a keyat most 512 bytes. Keys are compared as bytes, never by a collation.
a state valueat most 65,528 bytes: 64 KiB less the 8 the store keeps beside it.
a record's timeon a stream the engine witnesses, the time of the batch that commits it; any other time is refused. On a stream its source witnesses, the source's time, required, in a boot the stream's writer has placed, and never after the batch that carries it.
a tokenrequired on a stream declared with idempotency, refused on one declared without. One not above the stream's last is a duplicate, and writes nothing.
a mergeadd, max and min on 64-bit signed integers (an add that overflows is refused, never wrapped); union into a sorted set of byte strings; append; latest by a writer's stamp. A merge the stored value cannot take is refused, and so is its batch.
a typefields of bool, i8 to i64, u8 to u64, f32, f64, text, bytes, a time, a blob reference, lists, maps with a scalar key, and vectors of f32 or f16, nested at most 32 deep. Key fields are required, scalar, and not floats. A new version adds fields and changes none.

Requests

a messageat most 32 MiB, and at most 65,536 operations across its batches.
a readat most 4,096 records, or 16 MiB of them, a call; a program reads on from the last sequence it was given.
a rangeat most 4,096 keys a call, and it says whether more follow.
a history view by timeat most 4,096 rows a call, with a cursor to go on from.
a searchat most 1,024 hits, and at most 64 values required and 64 excluded.
a column scanat most 16 columns to group by, 64 aggregates, 2²⁰ groups, and conditions nested 64 deep. Floats are not summed.
following a graphat most 4,096 seeds and 2²⁰ rows answered.
an answer too large for one messagerefused, never cut short.
a watcheris cut off once 8 MiB of changes wait unread for it. A store holds at most 128 MiB of the views watches started from for watchers that have not read them; a watch past that is refused.
a backupread 8 MiB at a time, at most 2²⁰ files.

Connections

at once4,096 connections across every store, or fewer when magid's file-descriptor limit is lower. An eighth of them is kept for the system's store.
per accountan eighth of the users' share.
saying who it isa connection that has not said Hello within 5 seconds is let go.
the system's storereached only through its own socket, which the security policy opens to the journal's tail, the administrator and the system's own managers; no other service reaches it, root or not.

Rates and times

a lazy batchsynced within a second, five seconds under the saver power profile.
a group or sync batchanswered when an fdatasync covering it returns.
a store nobody usesclosed after five minutes with no request, no watcher and no writer attached; fifteen seconds under saver. The next request opens it again.
idlemagid sets no timer, and wakes for nothing.
a reopened storereplays at most about the last 8 MiB of its log, whatever has been written.
a redactionholds the store's writer while it rewrites the store's tables; batches that arrive meanwhile wait.
a backupholds the writer only for a checkpoint. A redaction is refused while one is being taken.

What a store does not do

  • Write across stores. A batch is atomic within one store, and nothing orders two stores' batches against each other.
  • Compress. A store's files are not compressed by the filesystem; a mount that forces compression ignores that.
  • Sign. Streams are declared unsigned; signing a stream's head is on the roadmap.
  • Replicate. A store lives on one machine. A fleet's record is on the roadmap.
